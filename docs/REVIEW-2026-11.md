# MGA League app: pre-publication review (November 2026)

> **Status, 1 November 2026:** everything in this review has been implemented except section 2.10
> (printable scorecards), which the league decided against: the point of the app is to replace paper.
> Decisions taken with the league: any signed-in member may enter scores for any matchup; the starting
> handicap stays a 9-hole figure; sign-in uses a 4 to 6 digit PIN; the default playoff format is 10 teams
> with a play-in, adjustable on the League tab. The sections below are kept as the record of why.

Scope: `public/index.html` (759 lines, the whole front end), `netlify/functions/api.mjs` (the server),
`SETUP-GUIDE.md`. Reviewed by reading every line and by running the app locally against a stub of the
API with 10 teams, 40 players and 4 weeks of scores, at desktop (1280px) and phone (390px) widths.

Goal: publish for the start of the next league year. Items are grouped by what must change before
publishing, then usability for older users, then aesthetics, then code and data quality.
Each group is in priority order.

---

## 1. Must fix before publishing

### 1.1 Any signed-in player can delete everything (server trusts the whole document)
`api.mjs:118-127` accepts the client's entire league state and replaces what is stored. The only
things a non-Commissioner is blocked from changing (`commissionerOnlyChange`, `api.mjs:42-58`) are the
League Year list, the course, playoffs and roles. Everything else is open to every signed-in player:

- "Delete entire schedule" (`index.html:322`) and "Delete weeks in a date range" (`index.html:341`).
- Deleting any team (which deletes its players) or any player (`index.html:563`, `deleteTeam`).
- Renaming anyone, changing anyone's email, phone or starting handicap.
- Editing or clearing any other player's scores for any week.

In a league of 40 people aged 60+, the realistic risk is not malice but a mis-tap on a phone.
Recommendation: move to **server-applied operations** instead of whole-document replace. The client
sends small, named changes ("set score: week W, player P, hole 3 = 5", "add matchup", "delete week W")
and the server checks each one against the caller's role:

| Who | May do |
|---|---|
| Player | Enter or correct scores for their own matchup that week; RSVP for themselves; edit their own email and phone. |
| Captain | Everything a Player can, plus pairs, tee times and scores for their own team's matchups. |
| Commissioner | Everything, including deletes, roster, schedule generation, course, years, playoffs. |

This also fixes 1.2 and 1.3 below, because small operations can be merged instead of colliding.

### 1.2 Lost updates when two people save at once
`api.mjs:35-39` reads the state, checks the revision (`:121`), then writes state and meta as two
separate, unconditional blob writes. Two saves that both pass the revision check overwrite each other,
and a crash between the two writes leaves state and revision out of step. Also, every keystroke on a
scorecard triggers a save after 400 ms (`index.html:132`), so on a league night with two or three
people entering scores, the "Someone else saved first, redo your last change" message will be common,
and `load(true)` then throws away whatever the person had typed since the last successful save.

Recommendation: upgrade `@netlify/blobs` from `^8.1.0` to the current 11.x and use conditional writes
(`getWithMetadata` returns an `etag`; `set(..., { onlyIfMatch: etag })` fails instead of overwriting).
Keep revision and state in **one** blob so the write is atomic. Combined with 1.1, a conflict becomes
"apply my score change to the newest copy" rather than "throw away your work".

### 1.3 Anyone on the internet can claim an unclaimed roster account
`api.mjs:85-92`: the site is public, the Account tab lists every unclaimed name, and claiming needs no
code. Anyone who finds the URL can become "Bob C." and start editing. Before the roster is fully
claimed (the first few weeks), this is wide open.

Recommendation: a per-league **join code** the Commissioner shares (stored in `meta`, changeable from
the Roster tab), required on "Create account". Also rate-limit `claim` and `login` by IP (a counter in
the blob store keyed by IP and hour is enough).

### 1.4 "Reset login" does not sign the old session out
`api.mjs:112-116` deletes the PIN but tokens (`api.mjs:15-25`) stay valid for 180 days and carry only
the person id. Whoever held the old phone stays signed in after the reset, and after a new PIN is set,
two people are "Bob C." at once. Fix: store a `tokenVersion` per account in `creds`, embed it in the
token, and reject tokens whose version is stale. Bump it on reset.

### 1.5 No backup, no history, no undo
The entire league is one blob named `state`. One accidental "Delete entire schedule" or a bad
"Generate schedule" over existing weeks is unrecoverable. Recommendation, in order of effort:

1. On every save, also write `state-rev-<n>` and keep the last 200 (or last 30 days). Cheap, immediate.
2. "Download a backup" button on the Account tab for the Commissioner (JSON file), plus "Restore".
3. Soft delete for weeks and players (a `deleted` flag plus a "Recently deleted" list), which also
   gives you a true Undo.

### 1.6 Polling cost will exceed Netlify's free tier
`index.html:758` calls the API every 5 seconds for as long as a tab is open, including background tabs
and phones left on the leaderboard. 40 people with the page open for three hours on a league night is
roughly 28,000 function calls per night. Over a 20-week season plus playoffs and daily casual checks,
this passes the free tier's 125,000 invocations a month and will either cost money or stop working
mid-season.

Fix: poll only when `document.visibilityState === 'visible'`, poll every 5 s only on the Scorecard tab
and every 30 to 60 s elsewhere, send `If-None-Match` with the revision so the server can answer 304
with no body, and stop polling entirely after 30 minutes without interaction.

### 1.7 "New week" defaults to tomorrow's date after about 8 pm Eastern
`index.html:296` and the Scorecard "New week" box use `new Date().toISOString().slice(0,10)`, which is
the UTC date. League nights are evenings; after 8 pm EDT (7 pm EST) the default is tomorrow, and a
week created from the Scorecard tab will not match the generated schedule's date. Use a local-date
helper everywhere a date is defaulted.

### 1.8 Silent data deletion on load
`purgeUnassigned()` (`index.html:571`) runs on every load and drops any player whose team is blank,
along with their scores, and the next save makes that permanent. Nothing on screen says this happened.
Remove it; instead show unassigned players in a "No team" group on the Roster (the Scorecard already
has one).

### 1.9 Server does not validate the data it stores
Because the server stores whatever JSON it receives, a malformed save (or a future front-end bug) can
corrupt the league for everyone. Add a schema check (ids are strings, scores are integers 1 to 15 or
blank, dates are `YYYY-MM-DD`, teams referenced by players exist, and so on) and reject with a clear
message. `fmtDate` (`index.html:111`) also inserts a non-matching date string into HTML unescaped, so a
bad date is also a small cross-site-scripting hole for signed-in users.

## 2. Usability for players aged 60 and over

Judged against the usual guidance for older users: larger type, strong contrast, obvious controls,
plain words, no disappearing messages, no hidden navigation, forgiving mistakes.

### 2.1 Navigation is hidden on phones
Ten tabs in a horizontally scrolling strip with the scrollbar hidden (`index.html:13-17`). On a 390px
phone only **Leaderboard, Team Schedule, Scorecard** are visible and nothing hints that seven more exist.
Most players will never find Handicaps, Account or EOY Outing.

Recommendation:
- Show players a short list: **Standings, Schedule, Scorecard, Handicaps, My Account**.
- Put **Roster, Course, Playoffs, Insights, Outing** under a "League admin" or "More" button, and
  show them only to Captains and the Commissioner (the server already knows the role).
- On phones, use a fixed bottom bar with icon plus label for the four or five main tabs.
- Rename: "EOY Outing" to "Year-end Outing", "Insights" to "Stats", "Account" to "Sign in".

### 2.2 Scorecard entry on a phone
The scorecard is a 13-column table with 40px-wide number boxes. On a phone, holes 4 to 9, Total,
Handicap and Net are off-screen inside each card and require sideways scrolling that is easy to miss.
Each hole box is 32px tall; the usual minimum touch target is 44px.

Recommendation: a phone-first entry mode. Pick the player, then show holes **one per row** (Hole 1,
Par 4, a large score box, net shown beside it), or a 1 to 9 tap pad with big buttons. Keep the table for
desktop and for printing. Use `inputmode="numeric"` so phones open the number keypad, and
`autocomplete="off"`. Save per hole with a visible "Saved" tick next to the box, not a toast.

Also: strokes-received cells are only indicated by a faint yellow background. Print a small dot or
"●" in the cell as well (colour-blind safe, and visible in sunlight).

### 2.3 Text is too small and too faint
- Base size is 15px (`index.html:8`). Use 17 to 18px for body text, 20px for table numbers on the
  scorecard, and never below 14px. Table headers are 11px uppercase letter-spaced grey (`:26`) and
  helper text is 13px (`:21`).
- Grey helper text (`#93A399` on `#161F1A`) measures 6.4:1 contrast, which passes, but at 12 to 13px it
  reads as faint. Lift it to the body colour at a smaller weight instead of grey.
- Long explanatory paragraphs under every table (the Leaderboard note is 120 words). Move these behind
  a "How scoring works" link or a collapsed "?" panel, and write them as three or four short sentences.

### 2.4 Dark theme by default
The whole app is dark (`color-scheme:dark`, `:6`), which is hard to read outdoors on a phone at a golf
course and is the less familiar choice for this audience. Make **light the default**, with a dark
option under Account, and respect `prefers-color-scheme`.

### 2.5 Messages disappear before they can be read
`toast()` (`index.html:115`) shows every message for 3.5 seconds at the bottom of the screen, including
errors such as "Not saved" and "Someone else saved first, redo your last change". Older users often do
not look at the bottom of the screen, and 3.5 seconds is short. Keep success messages brief but make
**errors and warnings stay until dismissed**, place them near the thing that failed, and use a
coloured bar with an icon, not just a change of text.

### 2.6 Delete is too easy
`del()` (`index.html:213`) turns a Delete button into "Sure?" for three seconds. A habitual double-click
(common among older mouse users) deletes immediately. Replace with a real confirmation dialog that
names what will be removed ("Delete the week of Wed, May 27 and all 40 scores entered for it?") and has
a clearly separated Cancel button. Keep destructive buttons away from everyday buttons (today "Delete
week" sits next to the week-type dropdown and the print button).

### 2.7 Forms use placeholders instead of labels
Add team, Add player, first-time setup, sign-in and PIN fields all rely on placeholder text, which
vanishes when the person starts typing and is low-contrast before. Use visible `<label>` elements
above each field. Group the "Add player" row (five fields and a button on one line) into a small form
with labels, and make Email and Phone optional and clearly marked as such.

### 2.8 Wording
Spell things out on first sight: "Hcp" to "Handicap", "SI" to "Stroke index", "Pts" to "Points",
"Net vs par" to "Net score vs par", "Tot" to "Total", "RSVP" to "Coming?". Avoid "PIN" for a 6+
character text field; call it a password or make it a 4 to 6 digit number (older users expect a PIN to
be digits). Replace the emoji-only buttons (🖨️, ✏️, 🚫) with text: "Print sign-in sheet", "Change
pairs", "Record forfeit".

### 2.9 Signing in
The name-picker plus PIN flow is good for this audience (nothing to remember except the PIN). Improve
it by: keeping people signed in on their own phone (already 180 days, fine), adding a "Show PIN"
checkbox, and showing "Signed in as Jim B." permanently in the header with a Sign out link, so people
know whether their changes will save. Today, a signed-out person can type scores for a minute before
the first save fails.

### 2.10 Printing
Older leagues live on paper. The only printable today is a sign-in sheet. Add printable **scorecards
for the night** (one per matchup, pairs filled in, par and stroke index, strokes-received marked) and a
printable **standings and schedule** page. A `@media print` stylesheet for the main page would get most
of the way.

### 2.11 Leaderboard
Eleven columns, most of which are empty dashes in Scramble columns. For players, show Rank, Team,
Points, Record and a plain-text playoff status ("In", "Play-in", "Out") instead of colour alone. Put the
other statistics on the Stats tab. On phones, show only the first four columns.

## 3. Aesthetics

What works: a consistent palette, good typography pairing (Space Grotesk and Manrope), generous card
spacing, and a clear active-tab pill. It looks modern on desktop.

What to change:

- **Light default** (see 2.4). Keep the green accent; on white it will need a darker green
  (`#2F7D32` range) for buttons with white text, and a pale green for row highlights.
- **Identity.** "MGA" plus a generic ball logo tells a newcomer nothing. Use the league's full name
  in the header and the year under it, and make the ball logo optional.
- **Hierarchy.** Every card has the same weight. Give each tab one clear headline and put the primary
  action (Add week, Generate schedule, Sign in) as the only green button on screen. Today "Add team",
  "Add player", "Add week", "Add", "Generate", "Create" all compete.
- **Status colours.** The row tints (green, yellow, red) are too subtle on dark, and there is no legend.
  Add a legend chip row above the table and a text column (2.11).
- **Row density.** Table rows are 9px padded; at larger type, go to 12px and use zebra striping
  rather than borders alone.
- **Empty states.** The Playoffs tab shows two separate boxes saying similar things. Combine into one
  friendly empty state with the next step as a button.
- **Fonts from Google.** Fine, but self-host the two font files in `public/fonts` so the app does not
  depend on a third party and loads faster at the course.

## 4. Code and data quality

### 4.1 Structure
- The front end is one 759-line file of long single-line functions (some lines over 2,000
  characters). It works, but it is hard to change safely. Split into `app.js`, `calc.js` (scoring and
  handicaps), `views/*.js`, and `style.css`; no build step needed if you use `<script type="module">`.
- `R()` (`index.html:729`) rebuilds the whole page via `innerHTML` on every change and loses focus and
  scroll position. Fine for now, but if you split files, render each tab's card separately.
- Inline `onclick="..."` handlers with ids spliced into strings are fragile (an id containing a quote
  would break the page). Use `data-` attributes and `addEventListener`.
- Add a tiny test file for the pure functions: `H`, `holeStrokes`, `pairCalc`, `scrambleCalc`,
  `matchPoints`, `roundRobin`, `seedOrder`. These are the rules of the league; a change that silently
  shifts a handicap by one stroke is the kind of bug that causes arguments at the bar.

### 4.2 Scoring and handicap rules (check with the league before launch)
- Handicap floor is 0 (`index.html:` the `H` function), so no plus handicaps. Probably fine.
- Starting handicap on the Roster is on the **9-hole** scale, but the field is labelled "Start hcp"
  with max 18. Most members know their 18-hole handicap and will enter that. Label it "9-hole
  starting handicap" and show the conversion, or accept an 18-hole figure and halve it.
- Handicap uses the last 5 rounds at 90%, excluding Scramble and playoff weeks; rounds in weeks with
  no schedule still count. Confirm that incomplete rounds (fewer than 9 holes) should be ignored
  rather than counted as "no card" penalties.
- Playoff format is hard-wired to exactly 10 teams (`genSeeds` refuses otherwise). If the league ever
  has 8 or 12 teams the Playoffs tab is unusable. Make the bracket size follow the team count.
- `edP` (`index.html:587`) allows a blank player name.

### 4.3 Server
- `api.mjs:65` parses the body for every POST before routing; fine, but cap body size (1 MB) so a bad
  client cannot write a huge blob.
- Put `node_version` in `netlify.toml` (`[functions] node_bundler = "esbuild"`, and `NODE_VERSION`
  in `[build.environment]`) and commit a `package-lock.json`, so a Netlify default change next year does
  not break the deploy.
- The `/api/state` GET returns the list of account ids (`accounts`) and every player's email and phone
  to anyone, signed in or not. Strip email and phone from the public response and only send them to
  signed-in users on their own team or the Commissioner.

### 4.4 Database choice
For one league of 40 players and about 25 weeks, a single JSON document in Netlify Blobs is a reasonable
store, and I would **not** move to a relational database for next season. The problems are not scale;
they are the four in section 1 (whole-document writes, no conditional write, no history, no
validation). Fix those and Blobs will serve for years. Consider Netlify DB (Postgres) only if you later
want multiple leagues on one site, ad-hoc reporting, or per-player history across many seasons.

If you do stay on Blobs, lay the keys out like this:

| Key | Contents |
|---|---|
| `league` | `{ rev, state }` in one blob, written with `onlyIfMatch` |
| `league-rev-<n>` | previous versions, pruned to the last 200 |
| `creds` | PIN hashes, `tokenVersion`, lockout counters |
| `meta` | `setupDone`, join code, theme defaults |
| `ratelimit-<ip>-<hour>` | login and claim attempts |

### 4.5 Setup guide
Clear and well-pitched. Two additions: a "Before the first night" checklist (course entered, roster
complete, schedule generated, join code sent) and a "Night-of" one-page card for whoever enters scores.
Also tell the Commissioner how to take a backup once that button exists.

## 5. Suggested order of work before the new season

1. Server-applied operations with role checks (1.1), conditional writes and single-blob state (1.2),
   token versioning (1.4). This is the one block of work that changes the architecture; do it first.
2. Join code and rate limiting (1.3), validation (1.9), revision history and backup download (1.5).
3. Polling changes (1.6), local-date fix (1.7), remove silent purge (1.8).
4. Light theme, bigger type, labels, persistent error messages, real delete confirmations
   (2.3 to 2.7).
5. Phone navigation and phone scorecard entry (2.1, 2.2), leaderboard simplification (2.11).
6. Printable scorecards (2.10), wording pass (2.8), tests for the scoring functions (4.1).
7. Split the front end into files (4.1) once the behaviour is settled, so later fixes are safe.

Do a dry run with three or four real members on their own phones in December, including one person
who has never used the app, and watch them enter a full card without help.
